## <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Tarikul-Islam-Anik/Animated-Fluent-Emojis/master/Emojis/Objects/Memo.png" width="22" /> Intelligence Analysis

### Executive Summary
In August 2025, PhishDestroy detected a total of 3,788 phishing domains, a significant increase of 441.1% compared to the previous month, which recorded 700 domains. Of these, 2,051 domains remain live, while 1,737 have been neutralized, resulting in a kill rate of 45.9%. The most significant finding this month is the surge in malicious activity targeting the crypto sector, especially involving drainers like Angel Drainer, which accounted for 220 instances.

### Key Trends
- **Crypto/Drainer Landscape** — Angel Drainer led the drainer activity with 220 instances, followed by Wallet Connect Abuse (95), marking a substantial rise in crypto-targeting tactics compared to July.
- **Brand Targeting Shifts** — Ledger was the most targeted brand with 128 phishing domains, a notable increase, reflecting a shift towards crypto-related brands.
- **Geographic Hotspots** — The USA remains the top hotspot with 1,663 domains, followed by a significant number of unknown locations (476), indicating potential use of anonymizing services.
- **TLD Abuse Patterns** — The .com TLD was abused the most with 1,828 instances, a consistent pattern, while .xyz saw a rise to 177 domains.
- **Registrar Abuse Concentration** — Registrations through untraceable entities labeled as "N/A" topped the list with 319 domains, highlighting accountability challenges.
- **Detection/Response Metrics** — Average response time to takedown requests was 4,426.6 hours, showing a need for faster interventions compared to last month.

### Threat Actor Tactics
Threat actors continue to leverage sophisticated infrastructure for phishing operations, including fast-flux networks and bulletproof hosting services. Nameserver clustering has been observed, allowing actors to switch between compromised domains quickly to evade takedown efforts.

The evolution of drainers and phishing kits is evident, with Angel Drainer and Wallet Connect Abuse showing advanced techniques in wallet targeting. These kits now feature more sophisticated social engineering tactics, increasing their effectiveness in capturing sensitive crypto wallet information.

Evasion and obfuscation techniques remain a challenge for detection engines. The spread of vendor detections on VirusTotal indicates that while some engines like alphaMountain.ai and Fortinet are effective, gaps remain in others, allowing threat actors to bypass specific engines through obfuscation tactics and polymorphic malware.

### Registrar Accountability
The top five registrars implicated in phishing domain registrations this month include an untraceable entity "N/A" with 319 domains, NameSilo, LLC with 228, and NICENIC INTERNATIONAL GROUP CO., LIMITED with 191. These registrars collectively account for a significant portion of the phishing landscape, raising concerns about their registration processes.

Compared to last month, NameSilo, LLC has shown a slight increase in registrations, while registrars like WEBCC and OwnRegistrar, Inc. have maintained similar levels. New entrants in the top five include NICENIC INTERNATIONAL GROUP CO., LIMITED, indicating a shift in registrar abuse.

### Detection Landscape
VirusTotal data reveals that alphaMountain.ai leads with 1,667 detections, closely followed by Fortinet with 1,610. Despite strong performances from these engines, detection gaps persist, particularly with newer or more sophisticated phishing kits. This underscores the need for continuous improvement in detection capabilities to address evolving threats.

### Recommendations
- **For Security Teams** — Implement continuous monitoring of domain registrations and employ automated threat intelligence feeds to detect emerging phishing threats promptly.
- **For SOC Analysts** — Focus on improving incident response times and leverage threat intelligence platforms to prioritize high-risk alerts effectively.
- **For Registrars** — Enhance verification processes to prevent fraudulent registrations and collaborate with cybersecurity firms to identify and mitigate abuse.
- **For Browser Vendors** — Increase integration of phishing detection technologies within browsers to warn users of malicious sites proactively.
- **For Crypto Projects** — Educate users about common phishing tactics and encourage the use of hardware wallets to secure digital assets.
- **For End Users** — Stay informed about the latest phishing tactics, verify URLs before entering sensitive information, and enable multi-factor authentication wherever possible.
