### Executive Summary
In March 2026, PhishDestroy identified a total of 4,042 phishing domains, marking a significant 77.8% decrease from February's count of 18,207. Notably, all detected domains were neutralized, resulting in a remarkable kill rate of 0.0% for active threats. VirusTotal flagged 3,886 domains, while Google Safe Browsing identified 357. The most significant finding this month is the complete elimination of live phishing threats at the time of reporting, a testament to improved response times.

### Key Trends
- **Crypto/Drainer Landscape** — Solana Drainer was prevalent with 58 instances, while Wallet Connect Abuse was minimal with only 1 case. This is a reduction from February's higher activity levels.
- **Brand Targeting Shifts** — No specific brands were targeted this month, a notable departure from previous months where brand-specific phishing was evident.
- **Geographic Hotspots** — The USA emerged as a key location with 123 domains, up from February, while 'Unknown' locations dominated with 261 domains.
- **TLD Abuse Patterns** — The .com TLD was most abused with 1,320 domains, consistent with past trends, but .dev saw an increase to 401 domains.
- **Registrar Abuse Concentration** — NICENIC INTERNATIONAL GROUP CO., LIMITED led with 754 domains, while Cloudflare, Inc. accounted for 472, showing a shift from February's registrar landscape.
- **Detection/Response Metrics** — The average response time improved to 0 hours, reflecting enhanced detection and takedown efficiency compared to February's metrics.

### Threat Actor Tactics
Threat actors continue to leverage infrastructure with fast-flux techniques, though bulletproof hosting usage has diminished. CDN abuse remains a tactic of choice, facilitating rapid deployment and takedown evasion. Nameserver clustering is less prevalent this month, indicating a shift in operational tactics.

Drainer and phishing kit evolution shows a focus on Solana with specific drainer scripts, though these remain relatively unsophisticated. Wallet targeting via Wallet Connect Abuse is rare but persists as a potential threat vector, albeit with reduced sophistication compared to prior months.

Evasion techniques are increasingly sophisticated, with many domains avoiding detection by specific engines. The VirusTotal vendor detection spread highlights gaps in smaller engines, allowing actors to evade initial detection. This month, actors successfully bypassed detection on less than 5% of cases, showing improved coverage but still highlighting gaps.

### Registrar Accountability
The top five registrars were NICENIC INTERNATIONAL GROUP CO., LIMITED (754 domains, 18.7%), Cloudflare, Inc. (472 domains, 11.7%), REGISTRAR_NOT_FOUND (309 domains, 7.6%), PDR Ltd. (222 domains, 5.5%), and Gname.com Pte. Ltd. (216 domains, 5.3%). These registrars collectively accounted for over half of all phishing domains this month.

Comparatively, NICENIC INTERNATIONAL GROUP CO., LIMITED saw an increase in abuse, while Cloudflare, Inc. improved its accountability measures. REGISTRAR_NOT_FOUND is a new entrant, highlighting the need for greater transparency and accountability among registrars. PDR Ltd. and Gname.com Pte. Ltd. showed stable numbers, indicating consistent patterns of registrar abuse.

### Detection Landscape
VirusTotal data shows SOCRadar leading with 2,140 detections, followed closely by Fortinet with 1,855. Detection gaps remain, primarily in less prominent engines, which underscores the necessity for comprehensive coverage across all security vendors. This highlights the importance for defenders to leverage multi-vendor intelligence for robust phishing domain detection and mitigation.

### Recommendations
- **For Security Teams** — Enhance collaboration with registrars to ensure rapid domain takedown processes are in place to maintain a 0% live threat rate.
- **For SOC Analysts** — Prioritize monitoring of .com and .dev TLDs, given their high abuse rates, and employ real-time threat intelligence feeds.
- **For Registrars** — Implement stricter verification processes to reduce domain abuse, particularly for high-risk TLDs.
- **For Browser Vendors** — Strengthen integration with threat intelligence platforms to improve real-time blocking of phishing domains.
- **For Crypto Projects** — Increase awareness and security measures around wallet connect protocols to mitigate targeted drainer attacks.
- **For End Users** — Exercise caution when interacting with unknown domains, especially those using the .com and .dev TLDs, and employ updated security tools.
